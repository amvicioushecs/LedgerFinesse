**Short answer:** Invitation Homes agreed to a settlement order requiring it to turn over **$48 million** after the FTC sued in September 2024 over alleged undisclosed fees and unfair move-out charges. On March 11, 2026, the FTC announced it was mailing **444,131 checks totaling more than $47.2 million** to eligible renters who paid $45 or more in covered charges.

## Two numbers, one case

You'll see both figures, and both are correct:

| Figure | What it is | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **$48 million** | The amount the settlement order required Invitation Homes to turn over to compensate consumers | [FTC case page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/202-3170-invitation-homes-inc-ftc-v) |
| **More than $47.2 million** | The total of the checks the FTC announced it was sending in March 2026 | [FTC press release, Mar. 11, 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-sends-checks-totaling-more-472-million-consumers-deceived-invitation-homes-undisclosed-fees) |

The FTC hasn't published a per-check average, so we don't estimate one.

## What the FTC alleged

According to the [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-sends-checks-totaling-more-472-million-consumers-deceived-invitation-homes-undisclosed-fees), Invitation Homes, one of the largest single-family home landlords, is alleged to have:

- **Deceived applicants about lease costs.**
- **Charged undisclosed fees renters couldn't opt out of**, for "services" such as "smart home technology" and "utility management."
- **Failed to inspect homes** before residents moved in.
- **Unfairly withheld security deposits** and imposed move-out charges for normal wear-and-tear, damage that existed before move-in, and even renovations.

These are the FTC's allegations. The case was resolved by a settlement order, not a trial verdict.

## What the settlement requires

Beyond the $48 million, the FTC says the order requires Invitation Homes to **clearly disclose its leasing prices**, **set up policies and procedures to handle security deposit refunds fairly**, and stop the other conduct the FTC challenged.

The case was filed in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Georgia (Civil Action No. 1:24-cv-04280-SEG).

## Who was eligible for a check

Per the FTC's March 2026 announcement:

- Renters who paid Invitation Homes **$45 or more** in covered fees or charges **between January 2021 and September 2024**.
- People who **already received a credit or refund** from Invitation Homes are **not** eligible.
- Checks should be **cashed within 90 days**, as printed on the check.
- Questions go to the FTC's refund administrator, **Rust Consulting, Inc., at 800-804-6915**.

**Scam warning from the FTC:** "The Commission never requires people to pay money or provide account information to get a payment."

## What this means if you rent from someone else

This case is about one company, but the patterns are worth checking on any ledger:

1. **Recurring fees that weren't in the advertised price.** Look for "tech package," "smart home," "utility management," or "admin" lines and see whether your lease actually lists them.
2. **Move-out charges.** Compare deductions to your move-in condition report and photos. Normal wear-and-tear charges were one of the FTC's specific allegations here.
3. **Duplicates.** Watch for the same fee billed under two codes or in two months.
