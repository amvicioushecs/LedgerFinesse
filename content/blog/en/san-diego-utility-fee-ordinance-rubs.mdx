**Short answer:** Since August 17, 2025, City of San Diego landlords may charge tenants for City-provided water, sewer, stormwater, and trash only up to what the landlord actually pays. Tenants must get written notice. Within 10 calendar days of a written request, the landlord must hand over the bill and the allocation math, with any third-party billing fee listed separately.

## What the ordinance covers

San Diego Municipal Code [Chapter 9, Article 8, Division 12](https://docs.sandiego.gov/municode/MuniCodeChapter09/Ch09Art08Division12.pdf), "Residential Tenant Utility Fees," was adopted by Ordinance O-21987 and took effect **August 17, 2025**.

It covers **"utility services," defined as City-provided water, sewer, stormwater, or solid waste management services** (§98.1202). If a utility on your bill isn't provided by the City, this ordinance doesn't cover that charge. Gas and electric are examples, and so is trash picked up by a private hauler instead of the City.

## The four rules that matter on your ledger

**1. No markup (§98.1203(a)).** A landlord may pass through the City utility fee for services you use, but the charge "shall be no greater than the amount the landlord pays for the tenant's utility services."

**2. Written notice first (§98.1204).** The landlord must either put the utility fee in your lease or give all affected tenants written notice **at least 30 calendar days** before charging it. Landlords who were already charging before the ordinance had 90 days after it took effect to send notice. The notice must include specific "Equitable Fees and Right to Notice" language.

**3. Show the bill and the math (§98.1205).** Within **10 calendar days** of your written request, the landlord must give you a copy of the City utility bill or tax-roll billing report. If one City bill covers several units, which is the typical RUBS setup, the landlord must also give you **the calculations used to allocate it** to each tenant.

**4. Third-party billing fees must be itemized (§98.1206).** If a billing company's cost is passed on to you, that amount must be listed separately in the calculations, and you're entitled to invoices or receipts backing it up.

The ordinance also lets a tenant bring a civil action and seek injunctive and equitable relief plus monetary damages, including punitive damages (§98.1207). Landlords who use submeters under the state Civil Code's submetering chapter are exempt for the services that chapter covers (§98.1203(b)).

## What RUBS is, and why it's hard to check

RUBS stands for **Ratio Utility Billing System**. Instead of metering your unit, the landlord splits one building-wide bill with a formula, often based on unit count, square footage, bedrooms, or occupants. The National Consumer Law Center [explains](https://library.nclc.org/article/introduction-ratio-utility-billing-systems-tenant-advocates) that these formulas can be opaque and can pass along inflated charges or extra billing-company fees.

San Diego's ordinance helps because it gives you a right to **see the City bill and the allocation math**.

## How to check your San Diego ledger

1. **List every utility line** on your ledger: water, sewer, trash, "RUBS," "utility admin," "billing fee," and so on.
2. **Sort them**: City-provided water, sewer, stormwater, or trash fall under the ordinance. Anything else doesn't.
3. **Find your notice.** Is the fee in your lease, or did you get a written notice at least 30 days before it started?
4. **Ask in writing** for the City bill and the allocation calculations, and note the date. The 10-day clock runs from your written request.
5. **Compare** what you were charged to your share of what the landlord paid. Look for a markup, a billing fee that isn't itemized, or the same utility charged twice under different codes.
